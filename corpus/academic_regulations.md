# Northbridge Institute of Technology
## Academic Regulations and Examination Policy
**Policy ID:** NIT-AR-2026 | **Version:** 2026.1 | **Effective:** 1 July 2026

## 1. Purpose and Scope
These Academic Regulations establish the general rules governing undergraduate academic activity at Northbridge Institute of Technology (NIT). They cover registration, attendance, examinations, grading, academic progression, revaluation, supplementary examinations, academic records, and appeals. They apply to students enrolled in undergraduate programmes unless a programme-specific regulation expressly states otherwise. A programme-specific rule should be read together with these general regulations rather than assumed to replace every general requirement.

The purpose of the regulations is to provide predictable procedures while allowing the Institute to handle exceptional circumstances through designated authorities. Students are responsible for reading published notices and retaining documents relevant to their academic status.

## 2. Academic Calendar and Teaching Periods
The academic year normally consists of two regular semesters and an optional summer term. The Academic Office publishes the academic calendar before registration. The calendar identifies instructional periods, examination periods, registration windows, and major administrative deadlines.

A timetable may be modified for operational reasons. When the Academic Office publishes a revised timetable, students should use the revised date for the activity identified in the notice. A timetable change does not by itself change eligibility rules.

## 3. Course Registration
Students must register for courses during the registration period. During the first ten instructional days, a student may add a course with approval from the course instructor and department office. After the add period, changes require the approvals specified by the Academic Office.

A student may not normally register for a course whose listed prerequisite has not been completed. A department may approve a documented prerequisite waiver where the programme procedure permits one. A waiver must be recorded before the student is treated as satisfying the prerequisite.

Students should review their registration after the add/drop period. Errors should be reported promptly because an unrecorded registration can affect examination eligibility and academic records.

## 4. Attendance Requirements
Students are expected to attend lectures, laboratories, tutorials, seminars, and other compulsory academic activities. The standard minimum attendance requirement for appearing in a semester-end examination is **75% of scheduled instructional sessions in the course**.

Attendance is calculated separately for each course. Attendance from one course cannot be transferred to another course. The departmental attendance record is the primary administrative record unless corrected through the prescribed process.

Students below the standard threshold may submit an attendance representation within five working days after publication of the shortage list. The representation should identify the course, recorded percentage, dates or circumstances relied upon, and supporting evidence. Filing a representation does not automatically restore eligibility.

## 5. Attendance Adjustments
Approved institute-representative activities, officially assigned academic activities, and other absences specifically recognized by an institutional notice may be treated according to the instructions applicable to that activity.

Students should verify that approved adjustments have been reflected in the departmental record. An approval for one activity should not be assumed to apply to a different activity or course.

Medical attendance requests are handled under the Medical and Exceptional Circumstances Policy. Students must follow the medical documentation and approval process. For semester-end examination eligibility, the general 75% attendance threshold in Section 4 applies to all students, including students whose medical documentation has been approved; this regulation does not create a lower medical attendance threshold.

## 6. Examination Eligibility
A student who satisfies the applicable attendance requirement, has valid course registration, and meets other published prerequisites is ordinarily eligible to sit for the semester-end examination. Departments may publish an eligibility list before examinations.

A student whose name is absent from an eligibility list should contact the department before the deadline in the relevant notice. The department may verify attendance, registration, and approved exceptions before correcting the list.

## 7. Examination Absence
A student who misses an examination because of an approved medical emergency may request a deferred or make-up examination. The request should normally be submitted within three working days after the missed examination and should include the documentation requested by the Academic Office.

The standard examination-absence provision does not expressly establish an automatic right to a deferred examination for family weddings, vacations, ordinary personal travel, or social events. Students should not infer eligibility from the existence of medical absence procedures.

## 8. Examination Conduct
Students must carry the identification and materials specified in the examination notice. Unauthorized electronic devices, communication with other candidates, unauthorized material, impersonation, or deliberate disruption may constitute examination misconduct.

Invigilators may record incidents and refer them to the Academic Integrity Committee. A student may be required to provide a statement about an alleged incident.

## 9. Assessment and Grading
A course grade is determined using the assessment scheme approved for the course. The scheme may include assignments, laboratory work, quizzes, projects, mid-semester examinations, and a final examination. The syllabus should identify the weighting of major components.

Grades are recorded according to the Institute's grading scale. A student should review published results and report a suspected administrative error through the prescribed process.

## 10. Passing Requirements
Students must satisfy the minimum passing requirement for each registered course. Programme progression may depend on credits completed, failed courses, prerequisite relationships, and any progression conditions published for the programme.

A failed course may need to be repeated when required by the programme. The existence of a supplementary examination does not automatically mean that every failed course qualifies.

## 11. Revaluation and Result Review
Where a revaluation facility is offered, students may submit a request through the prescribed portal or office within the published period. The request should identify the course and examination.

Revaluation is a review mechanism and does not guarantee an increase in marks. If the Institute permits a correction for an administrative error, the corrected record should be reflected through the official result process.

## 12. Supplementary Examinations
The Institute may offer supplementary examinations for specified courses or categories of students. The relevant examination notice establishes eligibility, registration, fee requirements, and dates.

Students should not assume that a supplementary examination is available for every failed course or every semester. The published notice controls the particular offering.

## 13. Academic Appeals
A student may appeal an academic decision when an applicable regulation provides an appeal route. The appeal should identify the decision, course or examination, date, grounds, and supporting documentation.

An appellate authority may uphold a decision, modify it within its authority, or return it to the original office for reconsideration. An appeal does not automatically suspend an academic deadline unless the applicable procedure says so.

## 14. Academic Committee
The Academic Committee may issue interpretations of academic regulations and may approve limited exceptions where an applicable policy expressly grants that authority. The existence of a committee does not by itself mean that every academic requirement can be waived.

Committee decisions should identify the matter considered and any relief granted. A decision for one student should not automatically be treated as a general amendment to the regulations.

## 15. Academic Records
The Academic Office maintains official academic records. Students should use the designated process to request corrections. Supporting documents may be required.

Unofficial screenshots or messages should not be treated as a replacement for an official record when a formal procedure requires the official record.

## 16. Notices and Effective Dates
Official academic notices are published through the student portal or another channel designated by the Academic Office. A notice changing a date should identify the activity and applicable academic period.

Students are responsible for monitoring notices relevant to their programme. When two notices appear inconsistent, the student should request clarification from the responsible office rather than choose a provision solely because it is more favorable.

## 17. Working Days
A working day is a day on which the Institute's administrative offices are open. Weekends and officially declared institutional holidays are not normally working days unless a notice expressly states otherwise.

## 18. Student Responsibility
Students are responsible for monitoring registration, attendance, examination eligibility, results, and deadlines. Students should retain evidence of approved requests and official decisions.

## 19. Programme-Specific Rules
A programme may publish additional requirements for laboratories, clinical work, internships, projects, or professional accreditation. Such requirements must be communicated through an official programme document or notice.

## 20. Interpretation and Closing
These regulations provide a general academic framework. Where an official policy expressly grants a different procedure for a defined category, that provision should be read in its stated context. Where two applicable provisions appear incompatible and no precedence rule resolves them, students should seek an institutional determination.

## 21. Internship and Project Requirements
Students enrolled in programmes containing internships, capstone projects, laboratories, or fieldwork must satisfy the requirements published by the relevant department. A project deadline is not automatically extended because another course has granted an exception. Students should obtain written approval when an approved academic activity changes their expected participation.

## 22. Course Communication
Instructors may publish course materials and announcements through the learning platform designated by the Institute. Students are responsible for checking the platform during the teaching period. An informal message from another student is not an official amendment to an academic regulation.

## 23. Attendance Disputes
When a student believes an attendance entry is incorrect, the student should identify the course, date, and disputed session. The department may compare attendance registers and approved adjustment records. A correction should be recorded in the official attendance system when accepted.

## 24. Examination Timetables
The examination timetable identifies the course, date, time, and venue. Students must verify the final published timetable before each examination. A student who relies on an obsolete timetable may still be required to follow the final official schedule unless the Institute determines otherwise.

## 25. Examination Materials
Permitted materials are determined by the examination notice or course instructions. A material permitted in one examination should not automatically be assumed to be permitted in another. Students should ask the department when an instruction is unclear.

## 26. Results and Transcripts
Official results are released through the designated academic channel. Transcripts contain the academic record maintained by the Institute. Students should report discrepancies promptly and provide evidence supporting a requested correction.

## 27. Graduation Eligibility
Graduation requires completion of the credits and programme requirements applicable to the student's programme. Students should verify outstanding requirements with the department before the graduation application deadline. Attendance, examination, fee, and conduct requirements may each have separate consequences under their applicable policies.

## 28. Leave of Absence
A formal academic leave of absence may be available where an applicable programme or Institute process provides for it. Informal absence from classes should not be treated as an approved leave of absence. A student returning from approved leave must complete the re-entry procedure.

## 29. Student Identification
Students must use the identification method specified by the Institute for examinations and selected administrative processes. A lost identification card should be reported promptly. The procedure for temporary identification is determined by the responsible office.

## 30. Administrative Communication
When a student submits an academic request, the student should retain the acknowledgement, reference number, and final decision where provided. If no response is received by a published deadline, the student should contact the office identified in the procedure rather than assume approval.

## 31. Interpretation Principle
An answer generated from these regulations must distinguish a general rule from a conditional exception. A conditional exception should not be applied to a student who does not satisfy its stated conditions. Conversely, a general rule should not be presented as absolute when an expressly applicable exception exists.

## 32. Final Administrative Principle
Where the available documents do not establish a clear answer, students should be directed to the responsible institutional authority. The Institute's regulations are intended to support administration, not to authorize students or automated systems to invent precedence rules.
