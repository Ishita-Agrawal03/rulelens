# Northbridge Institute of Technology
## Student Code of Conduct
**Version:** 2026.1

## 1. Purpose
The Student Code of Conduct describes expected behavior and the general disciplinary process. Specific procedures may apply to academic integrity, hostel conduct, technology use, or other specialized matters.

## 2. Respectful Conduct
Students must treat peers, staff, visitors, and service personnel respectfully. Threatening, abusive, or seriously disruptive conduct may be referred under the disciplinary process.

## 3. Academic Integrity
Cheating, unauthorized collaboration, fabrication of records, impersonation, and deliberate misrepresentation in academic work may constitute academic misconduct.

## 4. Plagiarism
Submitting another person's work as one's own without appropriate acknowledgment may constitute plagiarism. The academic unit may apply its academic integrity procedure.

## 5. Harassment
Unwelcome conduct that materially interferes with another person's safety, participation, or learning environment may be investigated under the applicable procedure.

## 6. Property
Students must not deliberately damage Institute property or another person's property. Restitution may be considered where responsibility is established.

## 7. Digital Resources
Institute computing systems must be used according to technology-use rules. Unauthorized access, deliberate disruption, credential misuse, and misuse of institutional systems may be disciplinary matters.

## 8. Restricted Substances
Students must follow Institute and applicable legal requirements concerning restricted substances. Residence rules may impose additional restrictions.

## 9. Investigation
When a complaint is received, the responsible office may conduct a preliminary review. The student may be asked to provide a statement or relevant information.

## 10. Disciplinary Committee
A disciplinary committee may consider cases referred under the applicable procedure. The committee may issue findings or recommendations within its authority.

## 11. Possible Outcomes
Depending on the applicable rules and severity, outcomes may include warning, restitution, restriction, suspension, or another authorized measure.

## 12. Notice
Students should receive notice of an allegation and the process being used, subject to the requirements of the applicable procedure.

## 13. Appeals
Appeal rights depend on the decision and procedure. An appeal should identify the decision, grounds, and supporting information.

## 14. Academic and Hostel Processes
Academic, hostel, and conduct procedures may operate separately. A hostel decision does not automatically determine an academic outcome unless an applicable academic rule provides a connection.

## 15. False Information
Knowingly submitting materially false information in an institutional process may itself be a conduct concern.

## 16. Retaliation
Retaliation against a person for making a good-faith report or participating in an institutional process is prohibited.

## 17. Emergency Measures
Temporary safety measures may be taken when necessary to protect students or staff. Such measures do not by themselves establish a final disciplinary finding.

## 18. Student Responsibilities
Students should cooperate with legitimate institutional processes, keep contact details current, and comply with decisions issued under applicable authority.

## 19. Records
Disciplinary records should be maintained according to institutional recordkeeping procedures. Access should be limited according to applicable rules.

## 20. Closing
Students should consult the specific procedure referenced in a notice for detailed timelines, evidence standards, and appeal mechanisms.

## 21. Student Organizations
Student organizations must comply with registration, event, safety, and financial procedures applicable to recognized organizations. Organization activity does not exempt individual members from the Code of Conduct.

## 22. Events
Students organizing an Institute event may need prior approval for venue, timing, security, or other operational matters. Event approval does not authorize conduct prohibited by another policy.

## 23. Examination Misconduct Process
Where an examination incident is referred for disciplinary review, the student may be informed of the allegation and asked to provide a response under the applicable procedure. Academic consequences may also be determined under academic-integrity rules.

## 24. Technology Misuse
Students must not use institutional accounts to impersonate another person, interfere with services, or obtain unauthorized access. Technical access granted for coursework does not authorize access to unrelated systems.

## 25. Records and Evidence
Relevant records may include written statements, system logs, examination materials, photographs, or other information permitted by the applicable process. Students should follow instructions concerning submission of evidence.

## 26. Interim Measures
An authorized office may impose a temporary measure where necessary to protect safety or preserve an institutional process. An interim measure should not automatically be treated as a final finding.

## 27. Cooperation
Students are expected to respond to legitimate notices and requests under disciplinary procedures. Failure to respond may have consequences only where the applicable procedure provides for them.

## 28. Multiple Proceedings
A single incident may implicate academic, hostel, and conduct rules. Each authority should apply the procedure within its jurisdiction. One process should not automatically be assumed to decide every consequence.

## 29. Appeals and Deadlines
Where an appeal is available, the applicable notice should identify the deadline and submission channel. A student should retain proof of submission.

## 30. Retaliation and Good-Faith Reports
A student who makes a good-faith report should not be subjected to retaliation for making the report or participating honestly in a review.

## 31. Misuse of Complaints
Knowingly fabricating a complaint or evidence may itself constitute a conduct concern. A complaint that is not substantiated is not automatically a false complaint; intent and evidence matter.

## 32. Closing Principle
The Code of Conduct provides general expectations. For a specific allegation, the relevant specialized procedure and official notice should be consulted rather than relying on a generalized summary.
